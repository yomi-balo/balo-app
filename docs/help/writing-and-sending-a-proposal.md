A proposal is one page that you work through from top to bottom. Your draft saves as you go, so
you can leave and come back to it. When it's ready, you preview the proposal as the client will
read it, with the amounts you typed, and send it from there.

## One page, top to bottom

The sections always appear in the same order:

1. **Overview** — what you'll do and how you'll approach it.
2. **Milestones** — the stages of the work.
3. **Price and payments** — one price for the project and how it's paid. For **Time & materials**
   this section is called **Rates and billing**.
4. **Not included** — anything out of scope, so there are no surprises later. This one is optional.
5. **Attachments** — reference documents you want the client to see. This one is optional too.

Above the first section, the client's request is summarised for you. Choose **Show brief** to read
the full request while you write, and **Hide brief** to tuck it away again.

The first time you open a new proposal, Balo drafts the **Overview** and the **Milestones** from the
client's request, so you start with something to edit instead of a blank page. A moment of
**Drafting from {name}'s request…** shows while it works, then the draft appears with a
**Drafted from {name}'s request** label on the Overview and **Suggested from the brief** on the
Milestones. It never fills in prices, hours, dates or payment terms. Those are yours to set, and
drafted text never includes links. Edit anything you like, or choose **Clear** to empty a section
and start from scratch. You'll see **Overview cleared.** (or **Suggested milestones cleared.**)
with **Undo**, which puts the drafted section back. If you start typing in the **Overview** before
the draft arrives, Balo drops the whole draft so it never overwrites your words. The draft is made
once per request, so it won't change if you come back to the page later.

If Balo can't put a draft together, you'll see **Couldn't draft this one. Start from scratch.**
at the top of the **Overview**. Nothing is wrong with your proposal: the sections are simply empty,
and you write it yourself just as you would without a draft.

## The property bar

Three choices sit at the top of the page, because they shape everything below them:

- **Pricing method** — **Fixed price** or **Time & materials**. If you switch, your typed price
  and your hour estimates are kept, so switching back brings them back. A message confirms the
  switch and offers **Undo**.
- **Timeframe** — choose **Add timeframe**, then use the stepper to say roughly how many weeks the
  work will take. It's a duration, not a date. Choose **Remove timeframe** if you'd rather leave
  it open.
- **Terms** — Balo standard terms apply to every proposal. Choose **Attach your terms** to add
  your own alongside them. The chip changes to "Balo standard and your terms" once they're attached.

## Fixed price: price first

Under **Fixed price**, start with **Project price**: the full amount you'll receive for the whole
project. Experts tend to think "this project costs A$15,000", not "each deliverable costs this", so
that's where you begin.

Then choose **Payment schedule**. The presets are **One payment**, **30 / 70**, **50 / 50**,
**30 / 40 / 30** and **One per milestone**. **One per milestone** creates one payment for each of
your milestones, split in proportion to their amounts, or evenly if you haven't priced them, and it
keeps up as you add, remove, rename or reprice milestones. The preset you've chosen stays
highlighted until you edit a payment by hand.

You can edit the name and percentage of any payment, and choose **Add payment** to add another; it
starts with whatever percentage is left to assign. The payments need to add up to 100%.

## Pricing each milestone is optional

You don't have to put an amount on every milestone. By default the milestones are just a
description of the work, and the **Project price** covers the lot.

If you want amounts, turn on **Price each milestone** beneath the milestone list. Then:

- Every milestone needs an amount.
- The amounts must add up to your project price. The page shows how much is left to assign, or
  how far over you are. Once every milestone has an amount, **Set price to** updates the project
  price to match the total.

Turning **Price each milestone** off clears the amounts, and your project price stays the same.
A message offers **Undo** if you change your mind.

## Time & materials

Under **Time & materials**, you set an hourly rate and a deposit in **Rates and billing**, and
choose whether invoices go out **Fortnightly** or **Monthly**. Each milestone needs an hour
estimate, and the page shows the total hours and the estimated cost. Milestones are a guide, not a
cap.

## The checklist and Preview and send

On the right of the page, the checklist lists what's still missing, such as an overview, a project
price or a payment schedule that doesn't add up. Choose any item to jump straight to it.

**Preview and send** is always available. If something is missing, choosing it takes you to the
first thing to fix instead of sending anything. When everything is in place it opens the preview.
On a phone, the same button and a short count of what's left sit in a bar at the bottom of the
screen.

You can also press **Cmd+Enter** (Mac) or **Ctrl+Enter** to preview.

The preview shows the proposal the way the client will read it. Nothing is sent until you choose
**Send** there, or **Back to editing** to keep working.

## What the client sees

In the preview, the amounts are the figures you typed, and the preview says so: "Amounts are what
you receive." The client sees the price with Balo's service added, so the numbers on their screen
are higher than the ones on your page. You never need to work that out yourself.

If a milestone has no amount, none is shown for it.

Once you send, you'll see a confirmation. The client gets an email and a notification, and from
there it's their call.

## Revising after feedback

If the client asks for changes, you'll see their note beside the section it's about, with a
summary at the top of the page and a **Go to note** link. Update the proposal as needed, then
choose **Mark as addressed** on the note. You can bring the note back any time with **Show note**.

A revision isn't saved as you go: the page says "Not saved until you resend". Your changes go to
the client when you choose **Preview and resend**, then **Resend**. If you leave before that, the
version the client last saw stays as it was. You can't add or remove documents while revising: the
ones already attached carry over to the version you resend.
